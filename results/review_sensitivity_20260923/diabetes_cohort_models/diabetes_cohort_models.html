Type 2 diabetes cohort models

Catalog stamp 20260923. The tables below are the four ancestry models. The cohort in the mixed models is the sample-set id.

Scope. A row is one evaluation: one score, one sample set, one ancestry, one AUC, and one SE. It is not the Stage 1 average. A cohort random effect marks which of those rows share a group. It does not make the AUCs independent.

The dependence model is being chosen on type 2 diabetes first. The same choice would then be applied to breast cancer (European-only training, South Asian target; 11 scores, 10 on one sample set) and stroke excluding subarachnoid hemorrhage (multi-ancestry training, South Asian target; 10 scores on the same two sample sets).

Cell counts across Figure 1C

The notes' 15 of 28 matches the tables before commit 7a552c1. It is the count of comparisons in which every score sat on the same target sample set. Seven further comparisons had only one score. It is not the count of cells with exactly one target sample set (that was 18 of 28).

  • Current tables: 30 Figure 1C cells.
  • Current: every score shares one target sample set in 17 of the k≥2 cells.
  • Current: 19 of 30 cells have exactly one target sample set.
  • Current: 20 of 30 cells have exactly one target study name.

Inside a cell with one target cohort, a cohort variance cannot be estimated from the target side alone. A reply that puts this random effect on all 30 current comparisons would overclaim. The diabetes model below uses European and target AUC rows together, so the cohort term is estimable when several scores share a sample set.

Evaluation table used here

71 evaluation rows. 18 scores. 29 sample sets. 12 study names. Corrected logit values from auc_ci_to_logit(). AUC models use the delta-method SE (SE_logit × AUC × (1 − AUC)). Fits are separate inside each training bucket. European is the reference ancestry.

South Asian European-only target sample sets in this table:

  • PSS011749 — UK Biobank (UKB), 10 evaluation(s)
  • PSS004315 — UK Biobank (UKB), 1 evaluation(s)
  • PSS011249 — Genes & Health (G&H), 1 evaluation(s)
  • PSS011291 — UK Biobank (UKB), 1 evaluation(s)

What is not in the tables

The study-name cohort was fit and is stored in diabetes_model_coefficients.csv. It is not a second version of the sample-set model. Under that coding, every UK Biobank evaluation shares one intercept, including East Asian, European, and South Asian. European-only East Asian then moves from the Figure 1C value 0.0179 to -0.0043. That happens because the cohort label and the ancestry label are no longer separate.

metafor::rma.mv, on the AUC and on the logit, is the weight check. lmer rescales the published standard errors; rma.mv keeps them fixed. For European-only South Asian those two AUC fits are -0.0107 and -0.0111. The check does not agree in the sparser multi-ancestry cells, which is why those rows are not on this page. One Middle Eastern row has no cohort text and would enter a study-name fit as its own group.

In the multi-ancestry bucket a sample-set cohort variance is barely estimated: 21 rows, 19 sample sets, and 2 of those sets contain more than one score.

Cohort random effect, score fixed: lmer(AUC ~ ancestry + PGS + (1|cohort), weights = 1/SE^2)

The cohort is the sample-set id. Evaluations from the same sample set share an offset, and the score stays a fixed intercept. The largest European-only sample sets contain only one ancestry, so that offset and the ancestry coefficient compete for the same shift. Ten South Asian scores share PSS011749, and all of them are South Asian.

If dropping rows is preferred to modelling the sharing

Two reduction rules, counted on the diabetes evaluation table above. The page does not pick a rule.

  • One score per sample set (lowest PGS ID within sample set × ancestry): keeps 33 rows, drops 38 of 71.
  • Largest cohort when one score is evaluated more than once within ancestry: keeps 65 rows, drops 6 of 71.
If you would rather drop rows than model the sharing, which rule do you prefer: one score per sample set, or the largest cohort when one score is evaluated more than once?
